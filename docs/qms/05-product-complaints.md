# Product Complaints

## What it is
A product complaint is an external report that a marketed product or material has a quality, labeling, packaging or performance problem. The process is: receive, record, investigate, respond and learn.

## Why it exists
Complaints are the earliest warning of problems after release. Every complaint must be recorded and investigated, even if unfounded, and trends must be reviewed.

## Complaint vs adverse event
A complaint is about product quality (e.g. discolored tablets). An adverse event is about harm to a patient. One report can be both, and then both processes run in parallel.

## Who does what
- QA Officer / Quality Executive: logs and classifies the complaint, leads the investigation, approves the response, decides on deviation, CAPA or recall review.
- Production Manager: provides batch records and process data, helps find the cause, implements fixes.
- Others: Customer Service (first contact), QC (retained samples), Pharmacovigilance (patient harm), Regulatory Affairs (reporting).

## Triggers
Discolored or damaged product, foreign particles, wrong label or leaflet, broken seals, wrong count or strength, suspected lack of effect.

## Flow
1. Receive and collect details (product, batch, defect, sample)
2. Record with a unique number
3. Triage and classify: critical, major or minor; check for adverse events
4. Investigate (batch record, retained samples, other batches)
5. Determine root cause, or justify "not confirmed"
6. Actions: deviation, CAPA, batch impact, recall escalation if risk is wide
7. Written response to the complainant
8. Close and review trends

## Example 1: API (paracetamol)
A customer reports dark specks in a paracetamol API lot. QA classifies it as major. The Production Manager supplies the batch record and dryer cleaning logs. QC finds metal particles, and the root cause is worn dryer parts. The lot is quarantined, other lots from that dryer are checked, and a CAPA replaces the parts and adds a preventive inspection.

## Example 2: Raw material
A customer reports that an excipient lot has a particle size different from its certificate. Testing confirms it, and the cause is a missed sieving step on one lot, which also opens a deviation. The lot is replaced, records are corrected and operators are retrained through a CAPA.

## Links to other modules
A complaint can lead to a deviation, a CAPA, supplier action, an adverse event report or a recall.

## Key terms
- Complainant: the person or company reporting.
- Retained sample: stored sample of each batch used for investigations.
- Batch traceability: tracking a batch from raw materials to customer.
- Trend analysis: reviewing complaints over time for patterns.

## One-line summary
Product complaints turn external quality reports into recorded investigations, fixes and trend reviews, so problems found in the market are traced back to their cause.
