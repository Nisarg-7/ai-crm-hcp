# CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action)

## What it is
CAPA is the formal process for fixing the root cause of a quality problem and preventing it from recurring. Correction fixes the immediate problem; corrective action removes the cause of a problem that already happened; preventive action removes the cause of a potential problem.

## Why it exists
A deviation handles one event. CAPA makes sure the cause is fixed so the event doesn't repeat. Regulators check CAPA closely, and repeated issues with no effective CAPA are a common inspection finding.

## Sources
Deviations, OOS results, complaints, audit findings, adverse events, recalls, trend reviews. Not every deviation needs one: major, critical and recurring issues usually do.

## Who does what
- QA Officer / Quality Executive: raises the CAPA, reviews the plan, verifies completion and effectiveness, closes it.
- Production Manager: usually the action owner who implements the fixes and meets deadlines.
- Others: Engineering (equipment), QC (methods), Training (retraining), QA Head (approves major CAPAs).

## Flow
1. Initiate, linked to its source
2. Root cause analysis (5 Whys, fishbone)
3. Plan corrective and preventive actions with owners and due dates
4. Approve the plan
5. Implement
6. Document evidence
7. Effectiveness check after a set period
8. Close, or reopen if it failed

## Example 1: API (paracetamol)
After a reactor temperature deviation caused by a drifting sensor: replace and re-calibrate the sensor (corrective), move all reactor sensors to monthly calibration and add a drift alarm (preventive). Owner: Production Manager with Engineering. Effectiveness check: no temperature deviations on that reactor for 3 months.

## Example 2: Raw material
After an excipient arrived with the wrong grade on its label: reject the lot and obtain a replacement (corrective); add a label-versus-certificate check at warehouse receipt, update the SOP and train staff (preventive). The issue also feeds the supplier's evaluation. Effectiveness check: no receipt-check failures for 6 months.

## Key terms
- Root cause: the underlying reason, not the visible symptom.
- Effectiveness check: later proof that the CAPA worked.
- Action owner: person accountable for completing an action.
- Recurrence: the same problem returning, a sign of a weak CAPA.

## One-line summary
CAPA fixes the root cause of a quality event, assigns owners and deadlines, and is closed only after proof that the problem has not come back.
