# Adverse Event Reporting

## What it is
An adverse event (AE) is any unwanted medical occurrence in a patient who has used a medicinal product, whether or not the product is believed to be the cause. Collecting, assessing and reporting these events is called pharmacovigilance (PV).

## Why it exists
Trials are limited in size and time, so rare or long-term effects often appear only after launch. AE reporting is how companies and regulators detect safety signals and protect patients. Legal reporting timelines are strict.

## Key distinctions
- AE vs ADR: an AE assumes no cause; an ADR is an event where a drug link is suspected.
- Serious: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, birth defect or other medically significant outcome.
- Expected vs unexpected: expected means already listed in the product label.

## Who does what
- QA Officer / Quality Executive: makes sure any AE found in a complaint, deviation or supplier issue is forwarded to PV immediately, links the records, and investigates any quality cause.
- Production Manager: provides batch and process information when the AE may relate to manufacturing.
- Others: PV team (assessment and reporting), medical reviewers, Regulatory Affairs, Customer Service and field staff (often the first to hear of it).
- Any employee who hears of an AE must report it, usually within 24 hours.

## Triggers
A reaction, unexpected effect, lack of effect, medication error, overdose or use in pregnancy reported by a patient, doctor, pharmacist or rep, by phone, email, visit, social media or literature.

## Flow
1. Receive: identifiable patient, reporter, suspect product and event make a valid case
2. Forward to PV within 24 hours
3. Record with a case number
4. Follow up for missing details
5. Assess seriousness, expectedness and causality
6. Report to regulators within legal timelines (for example 15 days for serious unexpected cases)
7. Signal review across cases
8. Action: label update, doctor letter or withdrawal if a signal is confirmed
9. Close

## Example 1: API (paracetamol)
A pharmacist tells a rep that several patients had liver enzyme rises after tablets from one API lot. The rep reports to PV within 24 hours. PV classifies the cases as serious. QA links them to the batch and opens a complaint and quality investigation. The Production Manager supplies the batch record. Testing finds a toxic impurity above the limit, which becomes a critical deviation, a CAPA and a recall review.

## Example 2: Raw material
A patient reports an allergic rash. Investigation finds an excipient lot contaminated with a trace allergen by the supplier. QA opens a deviation and a supplier complaint, checks other lots, and the supplier moves to conditional status.

## Links to other modules
An AE can come from a complaint, trigger a deviation and CAPA, affect supplier status and lead to a recall.

## Key terms
- PV: pharmacovigilance, monitoring drug safety.
- ICSR: Individual Case Safety Report sent to regulators.
- Causality: likelihood that the drug caused the event.
- Signal: a pattern across cases suggesting a possible new risk.

## One-line summary
Adverse event reporting captures patient safety events from any source, assesses them and reports them to regulators within strict deadlines, so risks are found and acted on quickly.
