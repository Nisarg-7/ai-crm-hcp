# Deviation Management

## What it is
A deviation is any unplanned departure from an approved procedure, specification or parameter during manufacturing, testing, storage or handling. GMP requires every deviation to be recorded, investigated, assessed for impact and closed with QA approval.

## Why it exists
A batch made outside its approved conditions may be unsafe or ineffective. The deviation process proves the company noticed the event, judged the impact and made a documented decision (release, rework or reject).

## Types and severity
- Planned: pre-approved temporary change (e.g. backup reactor during maintenance).
- Unplanned: happens unexpectedly, classified as:
  - Minor: no impact on quality or safety (e.g. missed log signature, corrected same day).
  - Major: potential impact on quality (e.g. parameter out of range).
  - Critical: direct risk to patient safety or product quality (e.g. contamination, wrong material used).

## Who does what
- Production Manager: notices the event, reports it, applies containment (holds the batch) and helps find the cause.
- QA Officer / Quality Executive: owns the process. Classifies the deviation, leads the investigation, assesses batch impact and approves closure.
- Others: QC (lab results), Engineering (equipment), QA Head (signs off major and critical cases).

## Triggers
Equipment failure or calibration issue, out-of-range process parameter, wrong or expired material, missed batch record step, OOS lab result, utility failure, environmental excursion.

## Flow
1. Detect and report (within 24 hours)
2. Record with a unique number
3. Immediate containment (stop the batch, quarantine material)
4. QA classifies: minor, major or critical
5. Investigate root cause (5 Whys, fishbone)
6. Impact assessment (this and other batches or products)
7. Batch decision: release, rework or reject
8. Raise a CAPA if the cause could recur
9. QA review and closure (QA Head signs off major and critical)
10. Periodic trend review

## Example 1: API (paracetamol)
Reactor temperature runs 5 °C above the approved range for 40 minutes during crystallisation. The Production Manager records it and holds the batch. QA classifies it as major and investigates. Root cause: a drifting temperature sensor that was out of calibration. QC tests purity and impurities and the results meet specification, so QA approves release with a documented justification. A CAPA is opened to replace the sensor and tighten the calibration schedule.

## Example 2: Raw material
An excipient arrives with a certificate of analysis for the correct grade, but the container label shows a different grade, and one batch has already used it. QA classifies it as critical and quarantines the lot. The Production Manager identifies and holds the affected batch. Investigation finds a supplier labelling error and a missed check at warehouse receipt. The batch is rejected, a CAPA improves the receipt check, and a supplier complaint feeds into Supplier Management.

## Key terms
- Root cause analysis (RCA): finding why it happened, not just what happened.
- Impact assessment: judging which batches and products are affected.
- Containment: immediate action to stop the problem spreading.
- OOS (out of specification): a lab result outside the approved limit, which often triggers a deviation.

## One-line summary
A deviation is an unplanned departure from procedure that must be recorded, investigated, assessed and closed by QA before the batch decision is made.
