# Supplier Management

## What it is
Supplier Management is the process of approving, monitoring and controlling the suppliers of APIs, raw materials, excipients and packaging. The manufacturer remains responsible for quality even for materials it did not make.

## Why it exists
A bad starting material becomes a bad product. Suppliers must be qualified before purchase and monitored afterwards.

## Who does what
- QA Officer / Quality Executive: owns the process. Evaluates suppliers, reviews documents, runs audits, maintains the Approved Supplier List, decides status changes.
- Production Manager: gives shop-floor feedback on material performance and raises issues with supplied materials.
- Others: Procurement (buys only from approved suppliers), QC (incoming testing), Regulatory Affairs (documents).

## Triggers
New supplier proposed, periodic re-evaluation, failed incoming test, deviation or complaint traced to a supplier, audit finding, supplier process or site change.

## Flow
1. Identify the need
2. Initial assessment (questionnaire, certifications, history)
3. Risk classification (API supplier = high risk)
4. Qualification (audit, sample batch testing)
5. Approval: add to the Approved Supplier List, sign a quality agreement
6. Ongoing monitoring (COA check, incoming tests, performance tracking)
7. Periodic re-evaluation
8. Issues: supplier complaint and SCAR, often a deviation and CAPA internally
9. Status decision: approved, conditional, or disqualified

## Example 1: API supplier
Supplier X provides a key starting material for paracetamol API and is rated high risk. QA audits it and tests three sample batches, then adds it to the ASL. In month 8 one lot fails purity testing. QA issues a SCAR, X corrects a process drift, and the Production Manager confirms the next lots run normally. X stays on conditional status with extra testing for six months before returning to normal approval.

## Example 2: Raw material supplier
An excipient lot arrives with the wrong grade on its label. QA records a supplier complaint, quarantines the lot and requests an investigation. The supplier finds a packing-line labelling error. Because it is a repeat issue, QA re-audits the supplier and applies full identity testing on every lot until the audit passes.

## Links to other modules
A failed incoming lot can trigger a deviation, which leads to a CAPA that may include a supplier corrective action. Complaints and recalls traced to a material feed back into supplier status.

## Key terms
- ASL: Approved Supplier List.
- Quality agreement: contract defining each party's quality responsibilities.
- COA: Certificate of Analysis.
- SCAR: Supplier Corrective Action Request.
- Disqualification: removal from the ASL after serious or unresolved failures.

## One-line summary
Supplier Management proves suppliers are qualified before use and keeps them under watch afterwards, because the manufacturer owns the quality of everything it puts into its product.
