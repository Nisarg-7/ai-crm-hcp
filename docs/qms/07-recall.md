# Recall Management

## What it is
A recall is the removal or correction of a product already on the market because it may be unsafe, defective or non-compliant. It is the final safety net of the QMS.

## Why it exists
Once a product is distributed, the company no longer controls it. A recall protects patients by stopping use of the affected batches. Regulators require a documented procedure, and companies run mock recalls to prove they can trace and retrieve a batch quickly.

## Classification
- Class I: reasonable chance of serious harm or death (e.g. toxic impurity above the limit).
- Class II: temporary or reversible harm, remote chance of serious harm (e.g. failed dissolution).
- Class III: unlikely to cause harm (e.g. labeling error with no safety impact).
- Depth: wholesale, retail or consumer level.
- A market withdrawal is for minor reasons that do not violate regulations.

## Who does what
- QA Officer / Quality Executive: leads the recall, proposes class and scope, coordinates with regulators, oversees retrieval, verifies effectiveness and closes it.
- Production Manager: provides batch and line facts, supports root-cause investigation, quarantines stock, implements the fix.
- Others: Recall committee or senior management (decision), Regulatory Affairs (notifies authorities), Distribution (trace and retrieve), Pharmacovigilance and Medical (health-hazard assessment).

## Triggers
Complaints or adverse events showing real risk, failed stability or retest on released batches, a critical deviation found after release, a supplier notification, a regulator request, an audit finding.

## Flow
1. Detect the trigger
2. Escalate to senior management
3. Health-hazard assessment
4. Decide: recall or not, class, scope and depth
5. Notify authorities within required timelines
6. Stop distribution and quarantine stock
7. Trace and notify customers
8. Retrieve affected stock
9. Reconcile quantities made, shipped, recovered and destroyed
10. Investigate, raise a deviation and CAPA
11. Effectiveness check, report to the regulator, close

## Example 1: API (paracetamol)
A toxic impurity above the limit is found in one paracetamol API lot after patients show liver enzyme rises. QA escalates and the assessment rates it Class I. The Production Manager finds the lot went into two downstream batches and quarantines remaining stock. Regulatory Affairs notifies the authority and customers. All affected material is retrieved and reconciled. The root cause is an uncontrolled process change, so change control is tightened and operators retrained.

## Example 2: Raw material
An excipient supplier reports a possible trace allergen contamination in a lot. QA traces it to three finished batches and classifies the recall as Class II. The Production Manager holds unshipped stock, the shipped batches are recalled from pharmacies, the supplier moves to conditional status, and a CAPA adds an allergen check on incoming lots.

## Links to other modules
A recall is usually the end of a chain: complaints and adverse events raise the alarm, a deviation confirms the cause, a CAPA fixes it, and supplier management responds. Recall findings feed back into all of them.

## Key terms
- Health-hazard assessment: how dangerous the defect is.
- Batch traceability: finding every customer who received a batch.
- Reconciliation: matching quantities made, shipped, recovered and destroyed.
- Mock recall: a practice exercise to test traceability.
- Effectiveness check: confirming the recall reached everyone.

## One-line summary
A recall pulls unsafe or defective batches back from the market through a controlled, documented process, so patients are protected when earlier controls have failed.
